# Complete MIRA verification evidence

## Problem

Whole-text clipping of canonical JSON can remove the designated finding or comparison subject. The verifier also filters cited human approvals before prioritizing references. Missing evidence currently has no separate verification outcome.

## Decision

Bound the local MIRA projections by complete records after redaction, credential scrubbing, JSON encoding and frame escaping. Preserve the complete mandatory subject and cited evidence, including privately resolved approval/request records. Use an unavailable verdict when indispensable evidence cannot be supplied; retain the finding and record the reason for the existing Policy Engine and Gate. Prove equivalent consumer failures at their public provider boundary before repairing them.

## Alternatives considered

Candidate-first string clipping still breaks JSON and can omit necessary evidence. Increasing limits only moves the failure boundary. Treating missing context as a negative verdict silently suppresses potentially valid findings. Widening the global model surface unnecessarily exposes private approvals to THY. A generic prompt construction framework adds lifecycle and configuration that these local consumers do not need.

## Consequences

Model verdicts require complete mandatory context. Some oversized or unavailable evidence produces an observable retained finding rather than a verdict. An artifact registration establishes identity and digest, but this verifier has no artifact-content reader; artifact references therefore remain unavailable until content can be supplied. A metadata-only claim can cite the registration event. Approval projections retain actor, run and subject provenance. Redacted source identities cannot resolve model citations. Deterministic and preflight findings, policy rules, exact-call approvals, global canonical JSON, historical evidence and THY visibility remain unchanged.
